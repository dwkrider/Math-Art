# Circle Packing

![Circle Packing](../images/circle_packing.png)

## Overview

A **circle packing** realises a prescribed pattern of tangencies: given a
triangulation, it assigns a radius to every vertex so that the circles at the two
ends of each edge touch. The radii are not free — the combinatorics *determines*
the geometry — and that rigidity is the discrete analogue of the Riemann mapping
theorem, so a packing actually **computes conformal structure** rather than
merely drawing circles.

### Using it

1. **Add it** with Add ▸ Math Art ▸ Patterns ▸ Circle Packing.
2. **Pick the Pattern** — *Hexagonal Disc*, *Square Grid*, or *Sphere* — and its
   size (**Rings** around the centre, or **Grid Size**).
3. **Choose the Geometry and Boundary.** *Maximal Disc* gives the discrete
   Riemann map of the disc; *Prescribed* maps onto a lobed shape you set with
   **Lobes** and **Lobe Depth**; *Free* is the cheapest. Hyperbolic geometry is
   what makes the maximal packing fill the unit disc.
4. **Refine** — each **Refinement** pass subdivides and re‑packs, and under
   refinement the packing converges to the true conformal structure.
5. **Choose the Output** — *Discs*, *Rings*, or the *Tangency Graph* — and colour
   by radius, neighbour count, or boundary.

## How it works

Imagine you are told only *which* coins must touch which — a map of tangencies,
like a honeycomb where each cell names its neighbours — and asked to choose the
coin sizes so every prescribed touch actually happens with no gaps or overlaps.
The surprise is that you have almost no freedom: once the pattern of neighbours
is fixed, the sizes are forced, and so is the shape the whole packing settles
into. That forced shape is a discrete stand‑in for a *conformal map* — the
angle‑preserving deformations at the heart of complex analysis — which is why
circle packing is sometimes called "discrete analytic function theory."

**The problem.** A triangulation names the tangencies. A packing is an assignment
of a radius $r_v$ to each vertex such that for every edge the two circles are
externally tangent. Around an interior vertex, its ring of neighbours (its
*flower*) must close up — the angles subtended by successive neighbour pairs must
sum to exactly $2\pi$. That closing condition, imposed at every interior vertex
at once, is what pins the radii down to the ambient normalisation.

**Thurston's relaxation.** The radii are found iteratively in the
Collins–Stephenson formulation: sweep the interior vertices, and reset each
radius to the value that would make its own flower close, repeating until the
whole configuration settles. The per‑vertex update is the *Uniform Neighbour
Model* — a closed form, so there is no inner root‑find — and the sweep is
accelerated by a super‑step. Once the radii are known, the circles are *placed*
by walking the triangulation and fanning each vertex's neighbours around it at
the angles the radii dictate.

**Why it is conformal.** The *maximal* packing of the disc is the discrete
Riemann map: its boundary circles become horocycles internally tangent to the
unit circle, and prescribing the boundary radii instead maps the pattern
conformally onto a different shape. Crucially, hexagonal **refinement** —
subdividing and re‑packing — makes the discrete map converge to the genuine
conformal structure as the mesh fines (the Rodin–Sullivan theorem), so the
pictures are not merely suggestive of conformality; they approximate it provably.
