# Subdivision Tiling

![Subdivision Tiling](../images/subdivision_tiling.png)

## Overview

A **finite subdivision rule** replaces every tile by a fixed pattern of smaller
tiles and iterates. The headline case is Bowers & Stephenson's **pentagonal
rule** — one pentagon becomes six — which tiles the plane so that every pentagon
at every level is *conformally regular* (conformally equivalent to a regular
pentagon, corners preserved) even though no two of them are congruent. The tiles
are pentagons only *combinatorially*, so their actual shapes come from a circle
packing rather than from the rule.

### Using it

1. **Add it** with Add ▸ Math Art ▸ Patterns ▸ Subdivision Tiling.
2. **Pick the Rule** — *Pentagonal* (1→6 pentagons), *Barycentric* (1→6
   triangles), *Quadrilateral* (1→4), or *Triangle to Quads* — and the **Depth**
   (each pentagonal pass multiplies the tile count by six).
3. **Choose the Layout** — this is the point of the generator: *Conformal* is the
   correct realisation (the maximal hyperbolic packing, filling the disc),
   *Packed Plane* packs in the plane with a free boundary, and *Straight Line* is
   a cheap Tutte embedding that visibly degrades with depth.
4. **Style the output** — *Tiles* or *Ribbons*, with **Tile Gap** to open the
   joins and **Relief** to lift tiles out of the plane into a panel.

## How it works

Start with one tile and a rule that says "cut this tile into these smaller
tiles." Apply it, then apply it again to every new tile, and again — the tiling
gets finer without bound. The famous example turns one pentagon into six smaller
ones. But here is the catch that makes it interesting: you cannot actually draw
all those pentagons *regular* with straight edges — the geometry simply doesn't
fit in the plane that way. They are pentagons only in the sense of *which tile
touches which*. To give them honest shapes, you hand that touching pattern to a
circle packing, which finds the one conformally‑correct realisation.

**Combinatorial rule, packed geometry.** The subdivision rule is purely
combinatorial: it produces, at each depth, a complex describing which tiles share
which edges and vertices — but *no coordinates*. Because there is no straight‑line
realisation in which every tile is regular, the geometry cannot come from the
rule. Instead the generator adds a vertex at the barycentre of each tile,
triangulates, and **circle‑packs** that triangulation; each tile is then drawn
through the circle centres at its corners. This is the method of Cannon, Floyd &
Parry's *tilepack*, and under refinement the tiles converge to their true
conformal shapes.

**The three layouts, and why they differ.** *Conformal* uses the maximal packing
of the hyperbolic disc — the correct realisation, filling the unit disc, in which
every pentagon reads as conformally regular. *Packed Plane* packs in the
Euclidean plane with a free boundary. *Straight Line* skips the packing entirely
for a Tutte (barycentric) embedding — cheap, and it visibly degrades with depth,
which is exactly what shows why the packing is needed. Beyond the pentagonal
rule, the generator also ships barycentric, quadrilateral, and a two‑type
triangle‑to‑quad rule, all realised the same way.
