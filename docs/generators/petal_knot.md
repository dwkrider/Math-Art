# Petal Knot

## Overview

Add a petal (petaluma) knot: 2k+1 petals around a single
multi-crossing, the knot type given by the permutation of
strand heights through the centre.

A **petal projection** is a knot diagram with exactly **one** crossing point — every strand passes through the same spot, and the cord loops away from it in petals arranged like a daisy. Colin Adams and collaborators showed that *every* knot has such a projection; the fewest petals it needs is its **petal number**.

The consequence is startling. With all the crossings collapsed into a single point, the only information left is the order in which the strands stack there — so a knot is completely determined by one permutation of $\{1,\dots,2k+1\}$. The whole of knot theory, for that diagram, compresses into a list of integers.

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Knots & Curves ▸ Petal Knot*.
2. **Choose the Knot.** The named entries are verified petal permutations — **Unknot**, **Trefoil 3_1**, **Figure Eight 4_1**, **Cinquefoil 5_1**, **Three-Twist 5_2**, **Torus Knot T(3,4) = 8_19**, **Septafoil 7_1**, **Torus Knot T(4,5) = 10_124**. **Random (Petaluma)** draws a seeded random permutation (a random knot in the Petaluma model), and **Custom Permutation** lets you type your own.
3. **Feed the chosen mode.** For *Random*, **Petals** sets the petal count $2k+1$ (an even number is rounded up to odd) and **Seed** picks which random permutation; for *Custom*, **Permutation** is a space- or comma-separated list — it must be a permutation of $1..N$ with $N$ odd, and $N$ itself sets the petal count.
4. **Set the main shape knobs.** **Petal Radius** is the radius of the ring of petal tips, and **Height Spread** is the half-range of the strand heights stacked at the centre — raise it (or thin the tube) when there are many petals so the strands stay clear of one another. **Samples / Petal** controls resolution.
5. **Pick the output.** *Bezier / Poly / NURBS* curve or **Mesh Tube**; **Tube Radius** (keep it below half the centre gap, $\text{spread}/(N-1)$, so strands don't merge), **Tube Sides** and **Bevel Resolution** finish it.
6. **Read the report.** It states the petal count, the exact height permutation used, and the number of crossings that collapse at the centre, $\binom{N}{2}$ — the single multi-crossing resolved into ordinary transverse ones.

## How it works

**In plain terms.** Lay a loop of string flat on a table so that it always passes back through the **same single spot**, looping out and in again in petals arranged like a daisy. Seen from directly above, that is all a petal diagram is: one crossing point where every strand of the loop stacks up, and a ring of petals radiating from it. Flat on the table the strands would all pass through each other at that spot, which is impossible for real string — so you **lift each pass to its own height**, some threads riding high through the centre, some low, each dipping back to the tabletop out at its petal tip. Now it is a genuine knot in space, and here is the whole trick: because *everything* interesting happens at that one shared spot, the only thing that can possibly distinguish one knot from another is the **order in which the strands are stacked** there — a single list saying which pass is on top, which next, and so on. That list, a permutation, *is* the knot. The rest of this section makes the daisy, the lifting and the "permutation is the knot" claim precise.

**The shadow.** The flat diagram — the daisy seen from above — is a **rose curve**

$$r=\cos(N\theta),\qquad \theta\in[0,\pi),\qquad N=2k+1\ \text{odd},$$

whose $N$ petals are equally spaced, and whose $N$ passes through the origin run out along the straight lines at angles $(2j+1)\pi/(2N)$. The oddness of $N$ is not a detail but a necessity: for even $N$ the curve $r=\cos(N\theta)$ traces each petal **twice** over $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$, doubling every strand, whereas for odd $N$ the range $[0,\pi)$ already sweeps out all $N$ distinct petals exactly once.

**One cord, not several.** For the diagram to be *one* knot rather than a link of several loops, the traversal must thread every petal before returning home. Stepping from one petal tip to the next advances by an angular **stride** of $(N+1)/2$, and this stride is always coprime to $N$ (for odd $N$, $\gcd\!\big(\tfrac{N+1}{2},N\big)=1$). A stride coprime to $N$ generates the full cycle of $N$ positions — you land on every petal exactly once before closing up — so the shadow is a **single** closed cord, not two or more interlinked ones.

**Lifting into space.** A flat rose is not yet a knot: all $N$ strands genuinely meet at the origin. Each pass $j$ is therefore raised to a height of its own using the profile

$$z=h_j\,\sin^2(N\theta),$$

so that at the centre ($\sin^2(N\theta)=1$) the strand sits at height $h_j\propto\mathrm{perm}[j]$, and at every petal tip ($\sin(N\theta)=0$) it drops back to the base plane. Two properties make this the right lift. First, $\sin^2$ has **zero derivative** at its zeros, so as one pass finishes and the next begins at a tip they meet with matching slope — the assembled curve is $C^1$, rejoining smoothly rather than kinking. Second, giving the passes **distinct** heights $h_j$ pulls them apart at the origin, so the space curve no longer self-intersects: it is embedded, and its shadow straight down is exactly the petal rose we started from. (The heights are the permutation mapped affinely onto $[-\text{spread},+\text{spread}]$, which is why *Height Spread* controls how far apart the strands sit.)

**Why the permutation is the knot.** In an ordinary knot diagram the over/under information is spread across many independent crossings. Here it is concentrated: there is **one** crossing point, so the entire over/under structure of the knot is nothing but the single **stacking order** of the passes at that point. Change the permutation and you change which strand is on top of which — you change the knot; nothing else in the construction (the rose, the stride, the lift profile) carries any topological information at all. This is the compression the overview promised: the whole of a knot's diagram encoded in one permutation of $\{1,\dots,N\}$.

That the *specific* permutations shipped here really give the *named* knots is not assumed from the list — it is verified for exactly this geometry. The lifted space curve is projected along a **generic tilted direction**, resolving the single $N$-fold multi-crossing into $\binom{N}{2}$ ordinary transverse crossings, and the knot **determinant** $|\Delta(-1)|$ of the resulting diagram is computed by exact integer elimination and matched against the target knot. (Exhausting all $5040$ seven-petal permutations this way reproduces the published classification: with $7$ petals exactly the knots $0_1,3_1,4_1,5_1,5_2$ and $8_{19}$ occur.) The self-test re-checks every preset's determinant from scratch.
