# Dual Helix

![Dual Helix](../images/dual_helix.png)

## Overview

This generator builds a sculpture of two nested counter-rotating helices joined into one closed loop. An outer helix (radius blending join → outer → join) spirals down while an inner helix (join → inner → join) spirals back up, both with a cosine height profile, so the two ends meet exactly at the top and bottom. The loop closes whenever the two turn counts sum to a whole number of turns (auto-snapped by default). The form is inspired by Tom Lawton's *[Wonder](https://tomlawton.medium.com/tom-lawtons-wonder-d517d4d479b5)* — an endlessly flowing ribbon that spirals up and down like a torus of moving water — and the curve can be swept with an elliptical tube profile.

### Using it

There is no mode to choose here — the sculpture is always the same two-spiral loop — so you shape it entirely through its radii, its two turn counts and the tube profile.

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Knots & Curves ▸ Dual Helix*.
2. **Set the three radii.** **Outer Radius** is how far the descending outer spiral bulges out at mid-height, **Inner Radius** the (usually tighter) width of the ascending inner spiral, and **Join Radius** the shared radius where the two meet at the very top and bottom. **Half Height** is how far the form reaches above and below the centre.
3. **Choose the winding** with **Outer Turns** and **Inner Turns** — how many times each spiral wraps around the axis. With **Auto Close** on (the default) the inner count is nudged so the two turn counts sum to a whole number, which is the condition for the loop to join up seamlessly; turn it off to wind freely and leave an open ribbon.
4. **Reshape the vertical motion** with **Height Profile**, the exponent on the cosine rise-and-fall: 1 is the plain cosine of the original, higher values flatten the middle and steepen the ends, lower values sharpen the middle into a point.
5. **Pick the output.** **Spline** builds the path as smooth **NURBS** (the original look), **Poly** or **Bezier**. When both **Tube Major Axis** and **Tube Minor Axis** are above 0 the curve is swept with an elliptical tube of those half-axes — optionally turned by **Profile Rotation** and refined by **Profile Resolution**; set either axis to 0 for a bare wire. **Segments Per Turn** and **Scale** finish the resolution and size.
6. **Read the report.** The status line prints the point count and whether the result is a `closed loop` or `open`, and if Auto Close adjusted the winding it tells you (`inner turns snapped to … to close the loop`).

## How it works

**In plain terms.** Imagine a spiral staircase winding down from the top of a tower to the bottom along the *outside* wall, and a second staircase winding back *up* through the middle, the two joined into one seamless ramp at the top and again at the bottom. Walk it and you loop forever: down the outside, up the inside, down the outside again. That is the whole sculpture — one endless closed path built from an outer downward spiral and an inner upward spiral that meet at both ends. The only real subtlety is making the ends line up perfectly, which is why the spirals are given matching radii where they meet and a whole number of total turns so the path closes into a loop instead of missing itself.

The sculpture is one closed curve made of two arcs. Along the **outer** arc, parameter $t\in[0,1]$, the radius blends between the *join* radius (at the ends) and the *outer* radius (at the middle) with a raised-cosine weight, the azimuth advances through the outer turn count, and the height follows a signed power of a cosine:

$$
w(t) = \tfrac{1 + \cos 2\pi t}{2},\qquad
r(t) = w\,r_{\text{join}} + (1-w)\,r_{\text{outer}},
$$

$$
\theta(t) = 2\pi\,N_{\text{outer}}\,t,\qquad
z(t) = h\;\mathrm{sgn}(c)\,|c|^{\,\kappa},\;\; c=\cos\pi t,
$$

giving points $\big(r\cos\theta,\; r\sin\theta,\; z\big)$. Here $h$ is the half-height and $\kappa$ the height-profile exponent ($\kappa=1$ recovers the plain cosine; larger flattens the middle, smaller sharpens it). The **inner** arc, parameter $s\in(0,1]$, does the mirror image: radius blends join → inner → join, azimuth continues from where the outer arc ended ($\theta_0 = 2\pi N_{\text{outer}}$) through the inner turn count, and the height is negated so the inner helix climbs back up:

$$z(s) = -h\;\mathrm{sgn}(\cos\pi s)\,|\cos\pi s|^{\,\kappa}.$$

At the join points ($t=0$ and $t=1$) both arcs are at radius $r_{\text{join}}$ and height $z=\pm h$ (the extremes of the profile), so the outer arc's bottom join meets the inner arc's start and the two form one continuous curve.

**Closure.** The inner helix arrives back at the outer start only when the total winding $N_{\text{outer}} + N_{\text{inner}}$ is a whole number of turns; the code detects this (modulo 1 within tolerance) and, if so, drops the duplicated final point and marks the spline cyclic. **Auto Close** rounds the sum to the nearest integer and snaps the inner turns to $\max(0.5,\ \text{round}(N_{\text{outer}}+N_{\text{inner}}) - N_{\text{outer}})$ so the loop always closes.

**Tube.** The curve is a NURBS (or Poly/Bezier) spline. When both tube axes are positive an **elliptical** Bézier profile of half-axes (major, minor), optionally rotated in the cross-section plane, is created as a bevel object and swept along the curve — reproducing the scaled bevel-circle of the original file.

## References
