# Seifert Surface

> ⚠️ **Experimental.** This generator is a work in progress — its
> options and output may change in future versions.

![Seifert Surface](../images/seifert.png)

## Overview

The Seifert generator builds the orientable spanning surface (**Seifert surface**) of any knot or link, entered as a **braid word**. Seifert's algorithm applied to a braid closure gives one disk per strand and one half-twisted band per crossing; the add-on stacks those disks coaxially (one per Seifert circle, ordered by their nesting) and joins the crossing rims with ±180° twisted ribbons — the SeifertView presentation. The surface boundary *is* the knot (optionally emitted as a bevelled tube curve), and the operator reports the strand, crossing, link-component and **genus** counts computed straight from the braid.

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Surfaces ▸ Seifert Surface*.
2. **Choose the knot.** The **Preset** menu holds ready-made knots and links (trefoil, figure-8, cinquefoil, the granny and square knots, Hopf / Solomon / Borromean links, …). Pick **Torus knot (p,q)** to dial the two torus parameters **Torus p** and **Torus q**, or **Custom braid** to type your own **Braid Word**.
3. **Read/write the Braid Word.** Letters name crossings: `A`/`a` is a right-/left-hand crossing of strands 1–2, `B`/`b` of strands 2–3, and so on; `A3` repeats, or use signed integers like `1 -2 1 -2`. This word alone fixes the surface's topology — every other control is finishing or looks.
4. **Pick the Surface.** **Seifert (oriented)** is the two-sided spanning surface; **Turnback state** and **Min-crosscap state** are non-orientable state surfaces of the same link (the min-crosscap search sweeps all $2^{c}$ states, so keep the crossing count small).
5. **Shape the finish** — how the crisp schematic relaxes into an organic form: **Relax Steps** (particle-membrane relaxation), **Refine Levels** (Catmull–Clark subdivision), **Fair Steps** + **Fair Strength** (mean-curvature fairing toward a minimal surface, knot rim pinned), and **Rim Smoothing** (extra passes along the boundary curve).
6. **Adjust the geometry** of the schematic itself (in units of the disk radius): **Disk Spacing** (stack height), **Band Width**, **Band Bulge**, plus the mesh-density knobs **Rim Samples/Foot**, **Band Samples** and **Disk Rings**.
7. **Choose the output.** **Scale** (1 fits the surface in a 2-unit cube); **Add Knot Curve** with **Knot Tube Radius** also lays a bevelled tube along the boundary knot.
8. **Read the report.** Each build prints the strand, crossing and link-component counts, the Euler characteristic $\chi$, and — for the orientable Seifert surface — the **genus** (for the non-orientable states, the crosscap number). These come straight from the braid, and the tests confirm the built mesh's own $V-E+F$ matches.
9. **Smooth it further (optional).** With a Seifert object selected, the companion **Minimize Surface** operator (F3 search) reruns the smoothing and reports the area reduction — offering the mean-curvature *Fair*, particle *Relax*, and the shrink-free bi-harmonic and conformalized-MCF fairings.

## How it works

**In plain terms.** A knot is a loop of string tangled up and then fused into a closed ring. Herbert Seifert proved in 1934 that *every* such loop, no matter how snarled, is the edge of a surface: you can always find a two-sided piece of "film" whose single boundary rim is exactly that knot — much as a soap film clings to a bent wire, except this film is guaranteed to exist for any knot at all. Seifert even gave a recipe for building one, and the recipe is easiest to run on a **braid**: picture $n$ parallel strings hanging down, and a *braid word* as a list of instructions to cross neighbouring strings over or under each other; fuse the bottom of the braid back to its top and the strings close up into a knot or link. Seifert's recipe then reads the surface straight off the braid — **one flat disk for each string, one twisted ribbon for each crossing** — and the number of holes in the resulting film (its *genus*) measures, in a precise sense, how genuinely tangled the knot is. Everything below is that recipe plus the work of turning its stack-of-plates schematic into a smooth, taut-looking surface.

**Combinatorics.** A **braid word** on $n$ strands is a sequence of generators $\sigma_i$ / $\sigma_i^{-1}$ (adjacent-strand exchanges), given as letters (`a` = $\sigma_1$, `A` = $\sigma_1^{-1}$) or signed integers. Its **closure** is a knot or link, and **Seifert's algorithm** on that closure produces one Seifert circle per strand and one twisted band per crossing. For a word of length $L$ on $n$ strands the generator computes the closure permutation, counts its cycles $\mu$ (the number of boundary / link components), and gets the genus of the connected surface from its Euler characteristic. Each disk contributes $+1$ and each band $-1$ to $\chi$, so

$$\chi = n - L, \qquad g = \frac{2 - \mu - \chi}{2},$$

the second identity being just $\chi = 2 - 2g - \mu$ (a sphere with $g$ handles and $\mu$ boundary holes punched in it) solved for $g$. This is why the genus is knowable *before any geometry is built* — it is a property of the braid word alone. The headless tests then confirm the generated mesh's own $V - E + F$ really equals $n - L$ for every preset, closing the loop between the abstract count and the actual triangulation.

**Geometry.** The $n$ disks are stacked **coaxially and at equal radius**: disk $i$ sits at height $z_i = \text{level}_i \cdot \text{Disk Spacing}$, where the level comes from a topological ordering of the Seifert circles (a circle nested inside another is placed above it). Crucially, each disk is meshed *not* as a polar fan — that would leave a single very-high-valence vertex at its centre, which both Catmull–Clark and the relaxation handle badly, showing up as a radial starburst crease no smoothing removes — but as an **O-grid**: a square core patch ringed by **Disk Rings** annular bands, so every vertex has valence four (bar four valence-three corners). For each crossing a band bridges the rims of the two neighbouring disks. Its centerline is a **cubic Bézier** that launches off one rim along a blend of the outward-radial and the vertical directions (so it bows out by **Band Bulge** before diving to the far disk), reaching out by $\text{Bulge}\cdot R + 0.42\,\text{span}$. The ribbon's cross-section is carried along that curve by a **rotation-minimising frame** — the frame that transports the section with no spurious twist of its own — and then exactly the twist needed to close the band is added on top, linearly in the sweep parameter. The half-turn count is chosen so the band arrives face-matched to its far disk: because both disks face $+z$ and the band makes a U-turn (which already flips the frame once), an *odd* number of geometric half-turns realises the $\pm 180°$ Seifert half-twist the algorithm demands. The band reuses its two feet's rim vertices one-to-one, so the ribbon welds seamlessly into both disks with no coincident duplicates, and every boundary vertex is flagged fixed for the optional pinned relaxation.

**Smoothing.** Two stages turn the crisp schematic into an organic surface, both leaning on the Minimal Surface Toolkit's mean-curvature machinery, and every step is required to preserve the braid's topology — `Mesh.info()` re-checks $(\chi,\ \text{boundaries},\ \text{orientability})$ after each.

- **Relax Steps** runs a particle-model membrane relaxation: each vertex feels a neighbour attraction and a pairwise repulsion, rounding the disks-and-bands cake out into a smooth blob. The fitted default pipeline uses only a *light* relaxation, because a long one drifts away from the true minimal surface — the particle model's equilibrium is not the same object as least area.
- After a **Catmull–Clark** subdivision (**Refine Levels**, each level roughly quadrupling the mesh) the surface is **faired**: **Fair Steps** of implicit mean-curvature fairing with the knot rim pinned flow it toward a discrete minimal surface — a taut film hung on the frozen knot — with **Fair Strength** the time step. **Rim Smoothing** adds fairing passes along the boundary curve itself.

**Minimize Surface operator** (`mesh.seifert_minimize`). A companion active on any object carrying a `braid` custom property (reach it from the F3 search menu); it reruns the smoothing and reports the area reduction, offering four methods:

- **Fair (minimal surface)** — implicit mean-curvature fairing with the rim pinned, flowing toward a minimal surface spanning the knot.
- **Relax (membrane)** — particle-model relaxation of the whole surface and its boundary together, with an optional **Pin Boundary** to freeze the knot and relax only the interior.
- **Fair (thin plate, no shrink)** — Botsch–Kobbelt **bi-harmonic** fairing: one equilibrium solve with the rim and its first ring locked, which smooths *without* the shrink-toward-the-rim that mean-curvature flow suffers and keeps the surface tangent-continuous at the rim.
- **Fair (conformalized MCF)** — Kazhdan–Solomon–Ben-Chen mean-curvature flow with the Laplacian frozen at the starting surface, robust against the neck-pinching and weight blow-up of long plain-MCF runs, converging to the harmonic span of the rim in the starting metric.
