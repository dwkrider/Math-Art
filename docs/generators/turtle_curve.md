# Turtle Curve

## Overview

Add a turtle curve: Koch teragons, folded dragons, FASS
curves, curves from combinatorial words, or spirolaterals.

One operator for the whole family of classical 2-D line-art constructions — Koch curves and their relatives, dragon curves from paper folding, space-filling curves, spirolaterals — lifted from line drawings into printable geometry.

Six modes, kept separate because there are genuinely six different ways to produce a turtle path, and collapsing them into one would misrepresent all of them. The clearest case: **node** rewriting and **edge** rewriting are not two styles of the same thing. The 2003 SIGGRAPH course notes show the same production giving *different figures* under the two composition rules, so a single "rewrite" mode would silently pick one and lose the other.

## How it works

**NODE** — node-rewriting [L-systems](lsystem.md), the classic path. A module's placement depends on the *whole chain* from the base of the structure up to it, so context accumulates along the branch.

**EDGE** — the Koch construction. A segment is replaced by a polyline whose position, orientation and scale come from **that segment alone**. This locality is what distinguishes it from node rewriting: the replacement knows nothing about where the segment sits in the figure. Signed teragon generators are applied to an $n$-gon initiator, the sign choosing which side of the segment the new detail bulges toward.

**FOLD** — paper folding. Fold a strip in half repeatedly, then unfold every crease to the same angle and read the sequence of left and right turns; at $90°$ this is the Heighway dragon. The unfolding parameter is **continuous** and keyframeable, so the curve visibly unfolds between generations rather than jumping.

**FASS** — space-**F**illing, self-**A**voiding, **S**imple and self-**S**imilar curves: Hilbert, Peano, Gosper and their kin, which approach filling a region without ever crossing themselves.

**WORD** — the turn sequence read off a combinatorial word rather than generated by rewriting, which reaches curves defined by number-theoretic sequences.

**SPIRO** — spirolaterals: walk a fixed repeating sequence of step lengths turning by a constant angle, optionally reversing at selected steps. The enumeration of which reversal sets give distinct figures is reduced using the **Whitney–Graustein invariant** — the turning number of a closed plane curve, which classifies such curves up to regular homotopy. Two reversal sets with the same invariant give the same figure up to deformation, so the invariant prunes the search rather than requiring every combination to be drawn and compared.

**Lifting into geometry.** The finished path becomes an object three ways: a bevelled tube, a filled flat island, or an extruded prism plate. Which are offered depends on **closure** — an open arc cannot be filled — and that decision comes from the Closed-Path Theorem in `lsystem/closure.py`, computed from the turn sequence rather than guessed from whether the endpoints happen to land near each other.
