# Fractal Knotwork

![Fractal Knotwork](../images/fractal_knotwork.png)

## Overview

Add a fractal knotwork (self-similar interlaced link on a Fathauer fractal tiling).

An infinite, self-similar over-under **link** woven across a fractal tiling. Take a [Fathauer f-tiling](fractal_tiling.md) — a patch of similar kites shrinking generation by generation toward a fractal boundary — and decorate it with classic medial knotwork: a crossing on every edge midpoint, cords routed around the faces, woven strictly one-over-one-under.

Because the substrate is self-similar, so is the interlace. Every deeper generation adds a finer halo of smaller cords, crossings and components around the previous ones — Celtic knotwork that never stops getting more detailed, in the spirit of Fathauer's iterated-substitution fractal knots.

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Fractals ▸ Fractal Knotwork*.
2. **Pick the Substrate** — the self-similar tiling the cords are woven over, one crossing per tiling edge. The three **Kite f-tilings** (6-, 8-, 12-fold) give the densest, most legible knotwork (6-fold densest of all); the **Golden-triangle** spiral (1- and 2-arm) and the **Equilateral (5-fold)** spiral are log-spiral tilings whose weave tapers from big outer tiles to tiny inner ones.
3. **Set Iterations** — the fractal depth; each generation adds a finer halo of smaller cords and crossings (5+ is heavy).
4. **Set Cord Width** — the cord thickness as a fraction of the *local* tile edge, so cords taper with the fractal scale rather than swamping the fine tiles.
5. **Choose Style** — *Angular* (straight mitred cords with sharp corners) or *Smooth* (Catmull-Rom curves); Smooth reveals **Smoothness**, the spline subdivisions per cord segment.
6. **Pick the Output** — *Ribbon Mesh* (flat interlaced tape), *Round Tube* (all-quad woven rope, revealing **Tube Sides**), or *Centerline Curves*. Ribbon reveals **Interlace**: *Flat Knotwork* breaks the under-cord at each crossing for a 2-D interlace, *Woven (3D)* lifts and dips the whole cord in $z$. Woven ribbons, tubes and curves all expose **Weave Height**, the $z$ amplitude — and a tube is always woven in $z$.
7. **Set Color By** (every output but Curve) — *Uniform*, *By Component* (a colour per closed loop of the link), or *Over/Under* (the two-tone alternating-weave checker).
8. **Set Scale** to fit the 2 m cube. The report prints the number of link components and the vertex/face counts.

## How it works

**In plain terms.** Think of Celtic knotwork: cords that weave over and under each other in an endless plait. Here the cords are laid down on an invisible grid of tiles. The rule is simple — drop a crossing at the middle of every tile edge, then thread cords around the tiles so each cord runs from one edge-midpoint to the next, and at every crossing alternate strictly one-over, one-under. Because the underlying grid is not an ordinary grid but a *shrinking* fractal tiling — big tiles at the centre, ever-smaller ones toward the rim — the plait keeps sprouting finer and finer cords as it approaches the edge. It is a braid that never stops adding detail: zoom in anywhere near the boundary and there is always another halo of tinier knots. Everything below is how those cords are traced, how the over-under is kept consistent, and how the cord thickness is made to shrink in step with the tiles.

**The substrate** is a kite f-tiling — the same construction as [Fractal Tiling](fractal_tiling.md), with rotational order 6, 8 or 12 — or one of the golden and equilateral log-spiral variants. The polygons are first *welded* into a shared-vertex cell complex (a safety-net splitter breaks any tile edge that a smaller tile's corner lands on, so the complex is genuinely edge-to-edge before the cord is traced; on the kite tilings it finds nothing to split).

**The medial construction.** Knotwork is placed by the classic mirror-curve rule: put one **crossing at every edge midpoint** of the tiling, and route the cords around the faces so each cord enters a face at one edge midpoint and leaves at the next. This is the same "polygons in contact" principle the [Islamic star patterns](islamic_pattern.md) use — the tiling is scaffolding that is never drawn, and only its edge midpoints leave a trace. Formally the cord lives on the *medial graph*: an interior edge is a crossing the cord passes straight through, a boundary edge reflects it into a U-turn, and the successor map on directed corner-segments is a **bijection** whose orbits partition every corner-segment into closed cords. That bijection is what guarantees the cords close up into loops rather than dangling.

Two properties then come free, and they are why the medial construction is the right one here:

- **Continuity.** Because contact points are shared edge midpoints, a cord leaving one face arrives exactly where the neighbouring face's cord begins. The interlace is continuous across the whole patch without any matching rules — the shared midpoint does the matching automatically.
- **Consistent weave.** The over/under is the alternating-knot assignment, solved with a **parity union-find**: each crossing carries one strand over and one under, and the sense must flip from each crossing to the next along a cord. On a tiling whose faces two-colour — which the f-tiling's edge-to-edge structure guarantees — those constraints are never frustrated, so the whole link is a genuine alternating diagram (mirror curves always are).

**Why it becomes self-similar.** The tiling's generations are similar copies scaled by $s=\tan(180°/k)$, and the decoration is defined *purely* in terms of tiling edges and faces. So the knotwork on generation $n+1$ is the knotwork on generation $n$ scaled by $s$ — the interlace inherits the substrate's self-similarity rather than being constructed recursively in its own right. To keep the picture faithful, each cord's width (and its woven $z$-amplitude) tracks the **local** edge length: it is `Cord Width` times that length, interpolated in *log-space* through the Catmull-Rom smoothing, so a fat cord over a big tile tapers geometrically into a fine cord over a fine one, exactly as in Fathauer's drawings.

**Components.** Each generation contributes its own closed cords, so the number of link components grows with depth. What is drawn is a finite truncation; the limit object is a link with infinitely many components, accumulating on the tiling's fractal boundary.

**Output** is ribbons, tubes or bare curves, with the over-under offset applied as a $z$ displacement smoothstepped between crossings — the same weave machinery as the [knot carpet](knot_carpet.md). The flat ribbon instead breaks the under-cord with a locally scaled gap; the round tube is swept with rotation-minimizing frames, welded closed all-quad, then rescaled ring by ring to the local cord radius so it stays a clean rope at every scale.

## References

- Robert W. Fathauer, "Fractal Knots Created by Iterative Substitution", Bridges Donostia: Mathematics, Music, Art, Architecture, Culture (2007), pp. 335-342 -- self-similar knots and links built by iterated substitution of a knotted motif.
- Robert W. Fathauer, "Fractal tilings based on kite- and dart- shaped prototiles" (Computers & Graphics 24, 2000) -- the f-tiling substrate reproduced by fractal_tiling_generator.
- Christian Mercat, "Les entrelacs des enluminures celtes" (2005) / "Knots and links from a graph" -- the medial construction of an interlaced knot on ANY planar graph (one crossing per edge), shared with celtic_knot_2d_generator.
- Slavik V. Jablan, "Mirror curves" and "Symmetry, Ornament and Modularity" (2002) -- mirror curves as alternating knot and link diagrams on arbitrary graphs.
