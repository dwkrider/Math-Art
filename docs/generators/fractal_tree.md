# Fractal Tree

![Fractal Tree](../images/fractal_tree.png)

## Overview

Parametric $n$-ary fractal trees, a botanist's bifurcating tree, and hanging mobiles — three views of one recursive branching, after the Mahler–Segerman ternary tree mobile (fig. 7-2 in Segerman's *Visualizing Mathematics with 3D Printing*).

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Plants & Growth ▸ Fractal Tree*. The same operator also appears under *Math Art ▸ Fractals* — either menu adds the identical generator.
2. **Pick the Mode.** **Tree** grows a trunk that splits into `Arity` children at every tip; **Honda** is Hisao Honda's botanical "tree-like body" (1971), a repeated two-way fork tuned by four numbers and shipped with his published plate presets; **Mobile** hangs the same branching structure downward as strings, spreader bars and sphere weights.
3. **Set the branching depth and spread.** **Arity** is how many children each node throws (2–5). In Tree mode **Depth** sets the number of generations and **Branch Angle** how far each child tilts from its parent; in Mobile mode **Levels** plays Depth's role. **Ratio** shrinks each generation's length and limb radius, and **Azimuth Twist** rotates each generation's fan — the golden angle (137.5°) gives crowns whose branches never line up.
4. **Dial the mode-specific controls.** *Honda* replaces the free branch angle with a **Plate** preset (I–VI, each a published parameter set) or **Custom**, which exposes the two branching angles **Theta 1 / Theta 2**, the two length ratios **R1 / R2**, and the **Divergence** roll used when a branching angle is zero; its **Width Exponent** sets the limb-thickness law ($n=2$ conserves cross-sectional area, $n=3$ is Murray's law). *Tree* adds **Leaf Spheres**, merging a small icosphere at every final tip. *Mobile* uses **Sphere Size** for the hanging weights.
5. **Choose the output.** **Trunk Length** and **Trunk Radius** set the overall size and the taper baseline; **Output** is Mesh or Curve, though whenever spheres are present (Mobile, or Tree with Leaf Spheres) the result is forced to Mesh. The model is centred and uniformly scaled to fit a 2 m cube.
6. **Read the report.** The operator prints the finished segment and sphere counts, and warns in the header if it had to clamp Depth or Levels down to stay under the segment budget (200,000 segments), or if it converted a curve to a mesh to carry spheres.

## How it works

**In plain terms.** A fractal tree is a tree in which every branch is just a smaller copy of the whole tree. Start with a trunk; at its tip, sprout a few branches; treat each branch as a new little trunk and do exactly the same thing again; repeat. Because one rule is applied at every scale, you get the endlessly self-similar look of a real tree — or of a head of broccoli, where every floret resembles the whole head. A **mobile** is that same picture turned upside down and hung from threads: each thread drops to a spreader bar whose arms each hang another thread. And **Honda's model** is a botanist's version that captures a real tree's silhouette using only four numbers. The word doing all the work is *recursion* — a rule that calls itself on smaller and smaller pieces until you tell it to stop.

**Tree mode — a recursion with a frame.** Starting from the origin with the trunk along $+Z$, the tree is built by a recursion that, at each tip of a branch of level $\ell$, emits a segment and then spawns $a=\text{arity}$ children. A child's direction is the parent direction $\mathbf{d}$ tilted by the branch angle $\phi$ and rolled to azimuth $\psi$ about $\mathbf{d}$:

$$\mathbf{d}_{\text{child}} = \sin\phi\,(\cos\psi\,\mathbf{u} + \sin\psi\,\mathbf{v}) + \cos\phi\,\mathbf{d},$$

where $(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})$ is a deterministic orthonormal frame perpendicular to $\mathbf{d}$. Read the two terms geometrically: $\cos\phi\,\mathbf{d}$ keeps the child pointed mostly the way its parent was going — so a small $\phi$ is a near-straight continuation, a large $\phi$ a sharp fork — while $\sin\phi\,(\cos\psi\,\mathbf{u}+\sin\psi\,\mathbf{v})$ is the sideways kick into the plane perpendicular to the parent, and $\psi$ swings that kick around the parent like the hand of a clock. The $k$-th child of generation $g$ uses azimuth

$$\psi_k = \frac{2\pi k}{a} + g\,\tau,$$

where the $2\pi k/a$ term spaces the $a$ children evenly around the circle and the $g\,\tau$ term offsets each whole generation by the azimuth twist $\tau$, so successive crowns are rotated relative to one another; a golden-angle $\tau$ makes them maximally non-aligning, the same trick a sunflower uses to keep florets from stacking. Lengths and radii scale geometrically: a level-$\ell$ segment has length $\propto \text{ratio}^{\ell}$ and per-point radii running $\text{ratio}^{\ell} \to \text{ratio}^{\ell+1}$, so a round bevel at the trunk radius yields continuously tapering limbs — and because a child's start radius equals its parent's end radius, the taper is unbroken across every fork. A full tree has

$$\sum_{g=0}^{\text{depth}} a^{g} = \frac{a^{\,\text{depth}+1}-1}{a-1}$$

segments, which grows exponentially in depth; Depth is clamped down (with a warning) to stay under the 200,000-segment budget. Optional leaves place a subdivided **icosphere** (unit icosahedron subdivided once → 42 verts, 80 faces) at each of the $a^{\text{depth}}$ final tips.

**Honda mode — bifurcation from four numbers.** Honda (1971) reduced a tree to repeated *bifurcation* — every branch forks into exactly two — described by four parameters: two branching angles $\theta_1,\theta_2$ and two contraction ratios $R_1,R_2$. Given a mother branch from $P_A$ to $P_B$, write $u=x_B-x_A$, $v=y_B-y_A$, $w=z_B-z_A$ and $L=\sqrt{u^2+v^2+w^2}$; a daughter of contraction $R$ and angle $t$ ends at

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= x_B + R\!\left(u\cos t - \tfrac{L\,v\sin t}{\sqrt{u^2+v^2}}\right),\\
y &= y_B + R\!\left(v\cos t + \tfrac{L\,u\sin t}{\sqrt{u^2+v^2}}\right),\\
z &= z_B + R\,w\cos t .
\end{aligned}
$$

The $\cos t$ part continues the mother's direction scaled by $R$, and the $\sin t$ part rotates by $t$ toward the horizontal unit vector $(-v,u,0)/\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$ — the direction normal to the vertical plane through the mother. Geometrically that means the branching plane contains the mother branch and takes the mother's direction as its steepest-gradient line (Honda's assumption (d)), while the daughter's length works out to exactly $R\,L$ (assumption (c)). The signs of $\theta_1,\theta_2$ alternate at every level, which is what produces the characteristic zig-zag. Two degenerate cases are handled as Honda handled them: a vertical mother (the trunk, where $\sqrt{u^2+v^2}\approx 0$) has no horizontal normal, so it forks in a plane chosen by the divergence angle $\alpha$; and a zero branching angle likewise falls back to a roll by $\alpha$ about the mother. Honda's three reported findings map straight onto the sliders — the branching angle sets the crown's **width**, the relative difference $R_1-R_2$ sets the degree of **apical dominance** (and so a conic versus a flat crown), and the ratio of the smaller angle to the branching angle sets **axiality**, how clearly a single main axis reads. A bifurcating tree of depth $d$ has $2^{\,d+1}-1$ segments.

**Mobile mode.** The same $n$-ary structure hangs downward under gravity: bars are horizontal, strings vertical, so the assembly balances instead of branching skyward. Each hang point drops a vertical string of length $L_0\cdot\text{ratio}^{g}$ (with $L_0$ the `trunk_len`) to a horizontal `arity`-armed spreader (arm length equal to the drop), generation $g$'s arms rotated by $g\,\tau$ in azimuth (default $\tau = 60°$ while the twist slider is untouched, so alternating spreaders do not collide). Final tips drop a half-length string to a sphere weight, and the bars taper slightly along their length.

**The width law.** Where limb thickness is split at a fork it follows the da Vinci / Murray law $w_{\text{parent}}^{\,n}=\sum w_{\text{child}}^{\,n}$: $n=2$ conserves cross-sectional area (da Vinci's observation that a limb's branches together match the trunk's cross-section) and $n=3$ is Murray's law (the exponent that minimises pumping work in a vascular network). In Honda mode the parent width is divided among the two children in proportion to their contraction ratios, so the dominant branch is visibly the thicker one; Tree mode uses the simpler $\text{ratio}^{\ell}$ taper.

**Fit and output.** All segment endpoints (and sphere extents) are bounding-boxed; the model is centered and uniformly scaled so the largest extent is 2 m, with the same `fit_scale` applied to the bevel so limb thickness stays in proportion. The curve is one POLY spline per segment carrying per-point radii; whenever spheres are present (Mobile, or Tree with leaves) the bevelled curve is converted to a mesh and the icosphere geometry merged in.
