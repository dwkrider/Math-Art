# Kleinian Limit Set

![Kleinian Limit Set](../images/kleinian.png)

## Overview

A **Kleinian limit set** is the fractal that a pair of Möbius transformations
accumulates on — the shapes from Mumford, Series & Wright's *Indra's Pearls*.
"Grandma's recipe" parameterises the whole family of once‑punctured‑torus groups
by two complex trace numbers, so the picture runs continuously from a wild
quasifuchsian Jordan curve to a gasket of tangent circles as those traces move
(the Apollonian gasket is one special member; this is the general case).

### Using it

1. **Add it** with Add ▸ Math Art ▸ Fractals ▸ Kleinian Limit Set.
2. **Pick the Output** — the three are genuinely different objects: *Limit Set*
   (the fractal as a bevel‑ready polyline), *Circle Orbit* (the group's images
   of a seed circle, as rings), or *Maskit Slice* (the boundary of the
   deformation space itself).
3. **Choose a Preset** — *Quasifuchsian*, *Gasket*, *Spiral*, *Near Circle*, or
   *Custom* — under the *Grandma's Recipe* or *Maskit* family. The presets set
   the traces for you; the named shapes are the landmarks of the family.
4. **Or go Custom** with **Trace A** and **Trace B** (each complex), or **Mu**
   for the Maskit family (which needs its imaginary part above 1).
5. **Control the detail** — **Detail** stops a search branch once its image
   shrinks below that size (lower resolves more of the set and costs more),
   bounded by **Word Depth** and a hard **Point Budget**.

## How it works

A Möbius transformation is a simple‑looking way of stirring the plane — really
the sphere — around: it slides, rotates, and scales, sending circles to circles.
Take *two* such stirs and apply them over and over in every possible order, and a
starting point goes on an endless tour. The places that tour piles up on — the
dust or curve it can never escape — is the **limit set**. Depending on how the
two transformations are tuned, that set can be a gently wrinkled loop, a wildly
fractal Jordan curve, or an infinite lacework of circles kissing circles.

**The group and its limit set.** Two Möbius transformations $a$ and $b$ generate
a group of transformations of the Riemann sphere. The limit set is the closure of
the orbit of (almost any) point under that group — the accumulation set of the
dynamics. The delicate part is choosing $a$ and $b$ so the group is *discrete*
(a Kleinian group) and represents a once‑punctured torus.

**Grandma's recipe.** Rather than choosing the matrices directly, the family is
parameterised by the two **traces** $\mathrm{tr}\,a$ and $\mathrm{tr}\,b$: given
those two complex numbers, Grandma's recipe (Mumford–Series–Wright, *Indra's
Pearls*, Box 21) reconstructs the generator pair, with the third trace fixed by
the Markov identity
$$\mathrm{tr}^2 a + \mathrm{tr}^2 b + \mathrm{tr}^2(ab) = \mathrm{tr}\,a\,\mathrm{tr}\,b\,\mathrm{tr}(ab).$$
So the whole deformation space is charted by two complex numbers, and moving them
is what carries the picture from quasifuchsian curve to gasket.

**Why depth‑first search, not a random walk.** A random iteration of the group
visits parts of the limit set exponentially rarely, leaving holes that never
fill. Instead the engine enumerates reduced words in $a, b, A, b$ systematically
by depth‑first search (the generators held in the cyclic order $a, B, A, b$), and
terminates a branch *geometrically* — once the image of a base shape has shrunk
below the **Detail** tolerance — rather than at a fixed word length. Those two
choices are exactly what make the output a continuous curve instead of a spray of
dots. The **Maskit Slice** output traces the boundary of the deformation space
itself, locating the cusp at every rational $p/q$ by Newton's method on the trace
polynomial.
