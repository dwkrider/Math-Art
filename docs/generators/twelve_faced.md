# Twelve-Faced Solid

![Twelve-Faced Solid](../images/twelve_faced.png)

## Overview

Add a twelve-faced solid: pyritohedron, tetartoid,
scalenohedron and their relatives.

The regular dodecahedron — twelve identical pentagons — is only the most famous of *many* twelve-faced solids. Following George Hart's survey, this generator builds the ones that carry a **shape parameter**, which is what makes them worth a slider rather than a stored vertex list: the **pyritohedron** and **tetartoid** (the irregular-pentagon forms that iron pyrite and cobaltite crystallise in), the **scalenohedron** and dipyramids (the triangle-faced forms, including calcite's), and two of Fedorov's space-filling **parallelohedra**, the elongated and trapezo-rhombic dodecahedra.

These are worth having because a single dial slides continuously between named solids — the pyritohedron's one parameter runs from the cube through the regular dodecahedron to the rhombic dodecahedron — and because several are the actual crystal habits of common minerals.

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Polyhedra ▸ Twelve-Faced Solid*.
2. **Pick the Solid.** Pyritohedron and Tetartoid are irregular-pentagon forms; Hexagonal Scalenohedron, Ditrigonal Dipyramid and Hexagonal Dipyramid are triangle-faced; Elongated Dodecahedron (8 rhombi + 4 hexagons) and Trapezo-Rhombic Dodecahedron (6 rhombi + 6 trapezoids) are the two parallelohedra.
3. **Turn the shape knob for that solid** — the redo panel shows only the ones that apply:
   - **Pyritohedron** → **Shape** $h$: $0$ is the cube, $1/\varphi \approx 0.618$ the regular dodecahedron, $1$ the rhombic dodecahedron.
   - **Tetartoid** → **Tilt** and **Skew**, the second and third components of the general face direction; Skew $=0$ puts the face plane back on a mirror and the solid stops being chiral.
   - **Hexagonal Scalenohedron** → **Zigzag** (how far alternate equator vertices rise and fall) and **Height**.
   - **Ditrigonal Dipyramid** → **Waist** (inner radius of the short alternate vertices) and **Height**.
   - **Hexagonal Dipyramid** → **Height** only.
   - **Elongated Dodecahedron** → **Stretch**, the depth of the inserted prism; $0$ gives the rhombic dodecahedron back unchanged.
   - **Trapezo-Rhombic Dodecahedron** takes no shape knob — it is a fixed close-packing cell.
4. **Choose the output** with **Style** (Solid, Leonardo, Struts, Ball and Stick, Wireframe, Face Segments) and **Scale**.
5. **Read the report.** Each build prints `V=… F=…`; every one of these solids has exactly twelve faces (the cube limit of the pyritohedron at $h = 0$ is the one exception, when opposite pentagon pairs flatten into six squares).

## How it works

**In plain terms.** When you think "twelve-sided solid" you probably picture the regular dodecahedron, with its dozen matching pentagons. But nature makes twelve-faced crystals in many other shapes: pyrite grows squashed pentagonal ones, cobaltite grows skewed lopsided ones, calcite grows sharp triangle-faced ones, and stacking spheres as tightly as possible carves out twelve-faced "packing cells." This generator builds a family of these, and most come with a slider or two so you can watch one shape flow into another — for instance nudging the pyrite form's dial takes it smoothly from a plain cube, through the perfect dodecahedron, to a diamond-faced solid.

**Two ways to build twelve faces.** The solids here are produced by two different methods, chosen to match how each one is naturally defined.

**Method 1 — plane orbits (pyritohedron, tetartoid).** Take a single plane, make copies of it under the **twelve rotations of the tetrahedral group** — the identity, three half-turns about the coordinate axes, and eight third-turns about the cube diagonals that cyclically permute $(x, y, z)$ — and then intersect all the resulting half-spaces $\lbrace\,n\cdot x \le |n|\,\rbrace$. The result is a convex solid whose every face is planar *by construction* and of which there are exactly twelve.

- The **pyritohedron** is the orbit of the plane through $(0, 1, h)$. The single dial $h$ runs the whole family:

  $$h = 0 \;\to\; \text{cube}, \qquad h = \tfrac{1}{\varphi} \;\to\; \text{regular dodecahedron}, \qquad h = 1 \;\to\; \text{rhombic dodecahedron},$$

  where $\varphi = (1+\sqrt5)/2$ is the golden ratio. At $h = 0$ opposite pentagons flatten into shared squares (the twelve faces become six); at $h = 1/\varphi$ the pentagons become exactly regular; at $h = 1$ each pentagon degenerates to a rhombus. Its symmetry is *pyritohedral* — the full tetrahedral group with mirrors, $T_h$ — which is why the pentagons are symmetric but not regular in general.
- The **tetartoid** is the orbit of a **general** direction $(a, b, c)$. A generic direction has no mirror plane through it, so its twelve-element orbit under the *rotations alone* gives a **chiral** solid of pure tetrahedral symmetry $T$ — the pentagonal-tritetrahedral form. Setting the third component to $0$ lays the face plane back onto a mirror, and the solid loses its handedness.

**Method 2 — dipyramids and packing cells (the triangle- and rhombus-faced solids).** These are assembled directly from vertices.

- A **dipyramid** places two apexes above and below a closed equatorial ring. The **hexagonal scalenohedron** takes a twelve-vertex ring whose height *zigzags*, alternate vertices sitting at $z = +\text{zig}$ and $z = -\text{zig}$; the alternating heights make every one of the twelve triangles **scalene** (all three edges unequal). The **ditrigonal dipyramid** instead alternates the ring in *radius* — $r = 1$ then $r = \text{waist}$ — giving twelve **isosceles** triangles, and the plain **hexagonal dipyramid** keeps the ring uniform for comparison.
- The **elongated dodecahedron** takes a rhombic dodecahedron, cuts it across a 4-fold axis, and inserts a prism of height $2\cdot\text{stretch}$: the four vertices in the cut plane each split into a pair and slide apart, turning $8$ rhombi $+\,4$ rhombi into $8$ rhombi $+\,4$ hexagons. At $\text{stretch} = 0$ the split pairs re-merge and the rhombic dodecahedron returns. It is one of Fedorov's five **parallelohedra** — convex solids that tile space by translation alone.
- The **trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron** is a **Voronoi cell of a sphere packing**: the region closer to one sphere than to any of its twelve touching neighbours, found as the half-space intersection against those neighbours. Six neighbours lie in the equatorial plane and three lie above, at radius $r = 1/\sqrt3$ and height $h = \sqrt{2/3}$; the only freedom is where the three *below* sit. Stacking them directly under the upper three (the $ABAB$ pattern of **hexagonal** close packing) yields this solid — $6$ rhombi $+\,6$ trapezoids — whereas rotating them $60°$ (the $ABCABC$ pattern of **cubic** close packing) would yield the ordinary rhombic dodecahedron. That single $60°$ choice is the whole difference between the two cells, which is why it is one flag in the code rather than two separate builders.
