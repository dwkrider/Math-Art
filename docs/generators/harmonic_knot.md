# Harmonic Knot

![Harmonic Knot](../images/harmonic_knot.png)

## Overview

Add a harmonic knot -- a Lissajous, Fourier, Chebyshev
or cube-billiard curve -- as a curve or tube mesh.

A **harmonic knot** is a closed space curve whose three coordinates are each a pure oscillation — sines, cosines, or polynomials in them. The remarkable fact is that such simple ingredients suffice: every knot can be written this way, and the different harmonic families each capture the whole of knot theory from a different direction.

The most striking members are the **Lissajous knots** (Bogle, Hearst, Jones and Stoilov, 1994) — the 3-D analogue of the oscilloscope figures — and the **billiard knots**, which are the trajectory of a ball bouncing forever inside a cube. Those two turn out to be exactly the same family, which is not obvious from either description.

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Knots & Curves ▸ Harmonic Knot*.
2. **Pick a Preset** for a ready-made, knot-type-verified curve — **Lissajous (3,2,7)**, **Fourier (1,1,2) Trefoil** (the $(2,3)$ torus knot), **Chebyshev Trefoil (3,4,5)**, or **Billiard (3,2,7)** — or **Custom** to drive everything by hand. Choosing a named preset overwrites the family, frequencies and phases below; *Custom* leaves whatever you have set.
3. **Choose the Family** (the wave in each coordinate): **Lissajous** (one cosine per axis), **Fourier** (cosine *sums*, with a second $z$-harmonic), **Chebyshev** (polynomial arcs closed by a bridge over the diagram), or **Billiard** (triangle waves — a ball bouncing in a cube).
4. **Set the main knobs.** **nx / ny / nz** are the three frequencies (for *Chebyshev*, the polynomial degrees $a,b,c$); keep them **pairwise coprime** or the strand retraces itself, which the operator warns about. **Phase X / Y / Z** shift the three waves and choose *which* knot appears; for *Chebyshev* only Phase Z is used, as the argument shift $\phi$. Setting all phases to zero also retraces the curve (warned).
5. **Mode-specific (Fourier only):** **Z Harmonic 2 Amp / Freq / Phase** add the second $z$-cosine that makes a Fourier-$(1,1,2)$ knot; amplitude $0$ collapses it back to a Lissajous curve.
6. **Choose the output.** **Samples** sets the point count; **Output** is a *Bezier / Poly / NURBS* curve or a **Mesh Tube**; **Tube Radius**, **Tube Sides** and **Bevel Resolution** set the thickness. The report names the family and frequency triple built and the number of samples.

## How it works

**In plain terms.** If you have ever seen the looping figures an oscilloscope draws when two tones are fed into it — the *Lissajous figures* — you have already met the flat ancestors of these knots. The idea is to let each of the three coordinates of a point just **wobble back and forth like a plucked string**: $x$ swings at one rate, $y$ at another, $z$ at a third, and as time runs the point they define chases out a closed loop in space. Choose the three rates carefully and that loop comes back to its start without ever meeting itself — a genuine knot, built from nothing but simple oscillations. The surprise, and the reason mathematicians care, is that *every* knot can be tied this way, and the same knots turn up in a completely different guise as the path of a **ball bouncing forever inside a cube**. The four "families" below are four flavours of wobble — pure cosines, sums of cosines, polynomials, and zig-zag triangle waves — and much of the beauty is watching them turn out to be the same thing wearing different clothes.

**Lissajous.** The purest version makes each coordinate a single cosine:

$$\big(\cos(n_x t+\phi_x),\ \cos(n_y t+\phi_y),\ \cos(n_z t+\phi_z)\big),\qquad t\in[0,2\pi),$$

so the point rides a Lissajous figure lifted into three dimensions. The frequencies $n_x,n_y,n_z$ must be **pairwise coprime**: if two of them shared a common factor $d$, that pair of coordinates would already repeat after $2\pi/d$, the whole curve would close early and **retrace itself** — a doubled strand, not a knot. With the frequencies coprime the curve visits a genuinely new position at each pass and closes only after the full period, giving one clean strand. The **phases** $\phi_x,\phi_y,\phi_z$ then slide the three oscillations relative to one another, and it is precisely this relative timing that decides *which* strands pass over which at the crossings — so the phases, not the frequencies alone, select the knot type. (Setting them all to zero makes the three cosines peak together and the curve degenerates onto a symmetric arc it retraces, which is why the operator warns.)

**Fourier.** Allow each coordinate to be a finite **sum** of cosines rather than just one, and the family grows. The important case adds a single extra harmonic in $z$, giving the *Fourier-$(1,1,2)$* knots — one cosine in $x$, one in $y$, two in $z$. Hoste proved the striking fact that **every torus knot** is a Fourier-$(1,1,2)$ knot, so this modest generalisation already reaches an entire infinite class. The built-in preset realises the $(2,3)$ trefoil this way; because its phases were found *numerically* rather than in closed form, the module does not simply trust them — its self-test resolves the sampled curve's crossings and computes the knot **determinant** $|\Delta(-1)|$, checking it equals $3$ on a small diagram, the fingerprint of the trefoil. That is verification of the topology, not of the fit.

**Chebyshev.** Trade the oscillations for **polynomials** — specifically the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind:

$$\big(T_a(s),\ T_b(s),\ T_c(s+\phi)\big),\qquad s\in[-1,1],$$

traced over the interval and then closed by a single bridging arc routed *above* the whole diagram, so the closure is an isotopically harmless overpass and the knot type is the standard closure of the long Chebyshev knot. **Every** knot arises this way (Koseleff–Pecker). And this is not a new family at all but the harmonics in disguise, through the defining identity

$$T_n(\cos t)=\cos(nt),$$

which says that on $[-1,1]$, substituting $s=\cos t$ turns each Chebyshev coordinate straight back into a cosine of an integer multiple of $t$ — a Lissajous curve, merely *reparametrised* so its samples bunch up near the turning points. The polynomial and trigonometric pictures are two views of one object.

**Billiard.** Keep the frequencies and phases but replace every cosine by the **triangle wave** of the same period — the symmetric zig-zag that rises and falls linearly between $+1$ and $-1$. A coordinate that ramps linearly and reflects at $\pm1$ is exactly a ball bouncing between two walls, so all three together trace the path of a **billiard ball ricocheting forever inside a cube**. That these are precisely the Lissajous knots is the theorem of Jones–Przytycki: **round the sharp corners** of each triangle wave smoothly back into a cosine and the trajectory deforms continuously *without any strand ever crossing through another*, so no crossing changes and the knot type is preserved. The billiard and Lissajous descriptions name the same knots by their corners and by their curves.

**Output.** As with the [torus knots](torus_knot.md), the curve can be emitted as a Bézier, poly or NURBS curve, or swept into a tube mesh.
