# Tight Knot

## Overview

Add a tight knot: the braid-table seed relaxed under the
self-avoiding tangent-point energy (knot type provably kept).

A **tight knot** is the shape a knot settles into when you pull a rope of fixed thickness as taut as it will go. Every knot has such an *ideal* form, and it is a genuine geometric invariant — the trefoil has a definite tight length, a definite shape, and a definite ropelength, independent of how you first tied it.

Reaching it is the hard part, and this generator does it honestly: it minimises the **tangent-point energy**, which diverges as the rope approaches touching itself. That divergence is the guarantee — the flow physically cannot push one strand through another, so the knot type is preserved throughout. A tangled unknot relaxes all the way back to a round circle; a trefoil never can.

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Knots & Curves ▸ Tight Knot*.
2. **Pick the Knot.** The **Knot** dropdown lists the whole Rolfsen table by `n.k` name, seeded from the same verified minimum braids as [Prime Knot](prime_knot.md); pick one and it tightens to that knot's ideal shape. Choose **Custom braid** to type your own word — the default `abaBcBC` is a deliberately tangled *unknot*, and watching it relax all the way to a round circle is the clearest demonstration that the flow is doing topology, not just smoothing.
3. **Enter a Braid Word** (Custom only). Lowercase `a..z` are the braid generators, uppercase `A..Z` their inverses; the word must close to a single knot (a link-closing word is rejected — use [Tight Link](tight_link.md)).
4. **Set the flow knobs.** **Curve Samples** is the polyline resolution: at or below 400 the exact dense solver runs, above it the faster lagged-factorization solver takes over automatically. **Tighten Iterations** is how many steps of the tightening flow to take — the flow usually converges in a few tens of steps, and `0` shows the raw seed before any tightening. **Mirror** builds the mirror image.
5. **Choose the Output and rope radius.** **Bezier / Poly / NURBS Curve** or **Mesh Tube**, as elsewhere. Leave **Radius From Thickness** on and the rope is drawn at exactly the measured **Gonzalez–Maddocks thickness** of the tightened curve — the fattest tube that still fits without self-overlap, which is precisely what "ideal knot" means; turn it off to set **Tube Radius** by hand. **Bevel Resolution** / **Tube Sides** set the cross-section, and **Scale** the overall size.
6. **Read the report.** On finishing, the operator prints the braid used, the energy before and after the flow ($E_0 \to E$, which should drop sharply), the number of steps actually run, and the tightened curve's **ropelength** (its length measured in units of its own thickness — the quantity being minimised).

## How it works

**In plain terms.** Take a knotted loop of soft rope and slowly pull it tight. As it tightens the rope wants to shorten, but it has a fixed thickness, so it cannot squeeze past the places where it presses against itself — those contacts stop it, and the knot settles into a snug, definite shape that is the same every time no matter how you started. That final shape is the *ideal knot*. To reproduce it in software you need one trick: a way to make "the rope may not pass through itself" into an actual force. The tool is the **tangent-point energy** — a running score that is small when the rope is comfortably spread out and grows without bound the instant two parts of the rope get close to touching. Because the score shoots off to infinity at contact, a process that only ever moves *downhill* in the score can never reach a self-crossing, and so can never change the knot. The whole method is: build that score, then let the rope slide downhill in it until it stops shrinking.

**The energy.** Concretely, for two points $x,y$ on the curve let $r(x,y)$ be the radius of the unique sphere that is tangent to the curve at $x$ and also passes through $y$. This *tangent-point radius* is large when the curve near $x$ points straight at $y$ (a gentle, glancing approach) and small when $y$ sits off to the side of $x$'s tangent line — exactly the configuration of two strands sliding past each other. The energy sums a high inverse power of it over all pairs,

$$E(\gamma) \;=\; \iint \frac{\big|P^{\perp}_{T(x)}(x-y)\big|^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\,\,ds_x\,ds_y,\qquad (\alpha,\beta)=(3,6),$$

where $P^{\perp}_{T(x)}$ projects onto the plane perpendicular to the unit tangent $T(x)$. With $\beta=2\alpha$ the integrand is exactly $\big(2/r(x,y)\big)^{\alpha}$, so as two strands approach contact $r\to0$ and $E\to\infty$. In the code the curve is a closed polyline and the integral is the discrete double sum $\sum_{i\neq j} K(x_i,x_j)\,\ell_i\ell_j$ over ordered vertex pairs, weighted by the segment lengths $\ell_i$.

That blow-up is the whole design. A gradient flow — repeatedly stepping in the direction that most decreases $E$ — cannot cross an infinite barrier, so no strand can pass through another and the knot type is an *invariant of the flow*, not something checked afterwards. The seed comes from the braid table and relaxes to the canonical tight shape; a deliberately tangled unknot such as the braid $abaBcBC$ untangles all the way to a round circle, which is the visible proof that the flow is doing topology and not merely smoothing.

**Constraints.** Minimising $E$ alone has two cheap cheats: the energy also falls if the curve simply shrinks toward a point, or drifts off to infinity. Both are ruled out by running the flow under two constraints — fixed total **length** and fixed **barycentre** (centre of mass) — so the only way left to lower the energy is to genuinely spread the rope out and round the knot. Numerically these appear as extra rows bordering the linear system solved at each step, projecting every move onto the surface where length and centroid are unchanged.

**Preconditioning.** A naive gradient step on a curve energy is dominated by the stiffest, shortest-wavelength wiggles: the sharpest little zig-zags carry the most energy, so a plain gradient spends all its effort jiggling them and the overall shape crawls. The descent is therefore *preconditioned* by a **fractional Sobolev–Slobodeckij ($H^s$) inner product**, of order $s=(\beta-1)/\alpha=5/3$ — a way of measuring the gradient that counts long, sweeping motions and tiny wiggles on a more equal footing. Solving the small bordered linear system with this metric rescales the raw gradient so that all wavelengths move at comparable rates, which is what makes the relaxation converge in a few tens of steps rather than thousands. Above 400 samples the same metric's factorisation is reused across several iterations (the *lagged-factorization* solver), trading a little accuracy per step for a large speed-up on long curves.

**Thickness.** Once the shape has settled, the rope needs a radius. It can be set from the measured **Gonzalez–Maddocks thickness** of the relaxed curve — the largest radius at which the swept tube remains embedded (never self-overlapping), which is the smaller of the tube's own radius of curvature and half the shortest distance between non-adjacent strands. Sweeping at exactly that radius renders the maximal-thickness rope of the tight shape, and the curve's length in units of that radius is its **ropelength**, the quantity the whole flow is minimising — which is why the report quotes it.

**Relation to the fast path.** The [prime knot](prime_knot.md) generator's smoothing-plus-repulsion relaxer remains available as a quick preview. It is faster but offers no topological guarantee — its repulsion is a finite penalty that a large step can jump — whereas this energy's barrier is genuinely infinite, so this is the version that provably cannot change the knot.

For multi-component curves see [Tight Link](tight_link.md), which adds inter-component terms and asserts the pairwise linking numbers unchanged.
