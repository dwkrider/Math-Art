# Rational / Pretzel Knot

## Overview

Add a rational (2-bridge), pretzel or twist knot as a
woven over/under diagram -- rope tube mesh or curve.

**Rational knots** — also called 2-bridge knots — are the best-understood family in knot theory, and they are classified by a fraction. John Conway's tangle calculus builds each one from a continued fraction of half-twists, and **Schubert's theorem** says that the resulting knot $K(p/q)$ is determined precisely by $p/q$: two continued fractions give the same knot exactly when their fractions agree (up to a standard equivalence). A knot, reduced to a rational number.

**Pretzel knots** are the sibling family, made by hanging several twisted strands side by side. Between them these account for most of the knots in the small tables, and their diagrams are drawn here as genuine over/under knotwork rather than as flat line art.

### Using it

1. **Add it** from *Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Math Art ▸ Knots & Curves ▸ Rational / Pretzel Knot*.
2. **Pick a Preset.** The named entries build tabulated small knots directly — Figure-Eight $4_1$, Trefoil $3_1$, $5_2$, the stevedore $6_1$, $7_2$, and three pretzels (including the $(2,2,2)$ three-component link). Choose **Custom** to type your own knot with the two fields below.
3. **For a Custom knot, choose the Family and Notation.** *Rational (2-bridge)* reads the Notation as a Conway continued fraction $[a_1\,a_2\,\dots]$; *Pretzel* reads it as the per-strand twist counts $P(p_1,\dots,p_n)$; *Twist Knot* takes a single $n$. **Notation** is just signed integers, space- or comma-separated (a negative entry twists the other way, giving the mirror image).
4. **Set the shape knobs.** **Twist Length** stretches or compresses each half-twist (the effective crossing size); **Region Spacing** sets the gap between pretzel columns; **Samples** is the point density along the strand; **Weave Depth** is how far the over-strand lifts above the under-strand at each crossing.
5. **Choose the Output.** *Mesh Tube* is a swept rope (with **Tube Sides**); *Bezier* / *Poly Curve* give a bevelled curve (with **Bevel Resolution**). **Tube Radius** sets the rope thickness, and **Color Components** gives each component of a multi-part link its own color.
6. **Read the report.** The status line prints the component count, the crossing count, and — for rational and twist knots — the classifying fraction $p/q$, so you can confirm you built the knot you meant to.

## How it works

**In plain terms.** A rational knot is a knot you can capture in a single fraction. Picture two parallel strands of rope. Twist them around each other a few times, give the pair a quarter-turn, twist again, turn, twist — a short recipe like "twist 2, turn, twist 2." John Conway's discovery is that this list of twist counts *is* a continued fraction, and the one number $p/q$ it adds up to names the knot completely: Schubert's theorem says two recipes tie the same knot exactly when they collapse to the same fraction. So the whole zoo of these knots is indexed by ordinary fractions, and reading off a knot's fraction is like reading off its serial number. A **pretzel** knot is the same twisting idea arranged differently — several separately-twisted ropes hung side by side, the way the dough strands of a pretzel run parallel — and a **twist** knot is the simplest case of all: one twisted region clasped shut at the end. Everything below turns that hands-on twisting picture into the exact plane diagram the code draws, then lifts it into 3-D so the crossings really go over and under.

**Growing a rational tangle.** Start from the trivial 0-tangle — two horizontal strands, untwisted — and read the continued fraction $[a_1,\dots,a_n]$ left to right. Entry $a_i$ adds $|a_i|$ signed half-twists, appended as a **horizontal** twist region on the right when $i$ is odd and a **vertical** one at the bottom when $i$ is even. Alternating the direction is Conway's construction, and geometrically it is what keeps the growing tangle from doubling back on itself: each new region is glued onto a fresh, empty face of the diagram. A positive $a_i$ twists one way, a negative one the other, which is exactly the mirror operation on that region. The fraction assembled by this recipe is

$$\frac{p}{q}=a_n+\cfrac{1}{a_{n-1}+\cfrac{1}{\ddots+\cfrac{1}{a_1}}},$$

read from the *inside out* — the last entry $a_n$ sits on top, the first entry $a_1$ at the bottom of the tower — so $[3]\to 3/1$ is the trefoil, $[2,2]\to 5/2$ the figure-eight, and $[3,2]\to 7/3$ the knot $5_2$. The numerator and denominator are not independent knobs: they are the single output of the whole twisting history, which is why one fraction can stand in for the entire diagram.

**Closing it up.** The finished tangle still has four loose ends dangling at its corners (NW, NE, SW, SE), so it is not yet a knot. Joining them in pairs closes it into a loop, and *which* pairing you use has to match how the recipe ended. When the last region was **horizontal** (an odd-length sequence) the code uses the **numerator** closure — arc the NW end over to NE above the diagram and SW to SE below. When the last region was **vertical** (even length) it uses the **denominator** closure instead — join NW–SW down the left and NE–SE down the right. The reason for the switch is that a vertical final region reports the *reciprocal* fraction $q/p$, and the denominator closure inverts it back, so both parities land on the same knot $K(p/q)$. By Schubert's theorem that knot is a single loop when $p$ is odd and splits into a **two-component link** when $p$ is even — which is why the $[2,2,2]\to 12/5$ pretzel-style inputs and even fractions produce genuinely linked pieces rather than one cord.

**The other two families.** A **pretzel** link $P(p_1,\dots,p_n)$ skips the alternating horizontal/vertical dance: it hangs $n$ **vertical** twist regions side by side, region $i$ carrying $p_i$ half-twists, then joins all the tops in a row and all the bottoms in a row with return arcs. Because every region is vertical, a pretzel can be a knot or a link depending on the parities of the $p_i$ (the $(2,2,2)$ preset is a three-component link — three interlocked loops). A **twist** knot $C(n,2)$ is the humblest rational knot of all: one twist region of $n$ crossings closed off by a two-crossing clasp. The code realises it as the rational tangle $[n,2]$, whose fraction works out to $(2n+1)/n$ — so $n=1$ is the trefoil, $n=2$ the figure-eight, $n=3$ the knot $5_2$, $n=4$ the stevedore $6_1$, $n=5$ is $7_2$, and the family marches on as $n$ grows.

**Why no crossing solver is needed.** In a general knot diagram, deciding which strand passes over at each crossing so that the weave stays consistent is a genuine global puzzle. Here it is free. Within any twist region the two strands swap places once per half-twist, and the **sign** of the entry fixes which one rides over — a positive region sends the descending strand over, a negative one under. A region of constant sign therefore *automatically* alternates over/under along each strand, so an all-positive continued fraction (or an all-positive pretzel) reproduces the classical alternating diagram, which the self-test verifies by checking that no two consecutive crossings on a strand have the same sense. Every over/under bit is thus read straight off the twist signs, with no consistency equation to solve.

Geometrically that over/under bit becomes a small vertical ($z$) offset, smoothstepped along each strand between one crossing and the next — the same weave profile the [knot carpet](knot_carpet.md) uses — so the flat diagram lifts into a genuinely embedded space curve. The tube mesh then reuses the knot carpet's proximity-welded, rotation-minimising sweep, which slides a circular cross-section along the strand without letting it spin, so the rope stays clean even where strands run close.

- C. C. Adams, "The Knot Book", W. H. Freeman, 1994 -- twist knots, pretzel links, rational tangles (ch. 2).
- D. Rolfsen, "Knots and Links", Publish or Perish, 1976 -- the knot table names (4_1, 5_2, 6_1, ...) used for the presets.
